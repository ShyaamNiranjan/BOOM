Outgrown Tally and Excel for operations? What comes next

8 min read · Updated 27 Sep 2026

Short answer: keep Tally, or whatever accounting software your CA uses, for books, GST and statutory work — that is what it is for. What most growing businesses outgrow is not the accounting software but the Excel sheets, WhatsApp groups and memory around it: orders, stock, dues follow-up and tasks. The next step is an operations layer the whole team works in every day, which hands clean data to the accountant.

What “outgrown Tally” usually means

When an owner says “we’ve outgrown Tally”, the accounts are usually fine. The accountant posts entries, GST gets filed, the CA gets the books at year-end. What has broken is everything that happens before an entry reaches the accountant.

Tally includes inventory and order features, and plenty of businesses use them well. In many Indian SMEs, though, it is operated by one accountant, and the sales team, dispatch, godown and owner do not work in it. The operational truth lives somewhere else:

  • Orders arrive on WhatsApp and phone and are written into a sheet — or not.
  • Stock is in an Excel file that the godown updates “when there is time”.
  • Customer dues are in the accountant’s system, but the salesman promising the next dispatch cannot see them.
  • Supplier payments are tracked in the owner’s head and a diary.
  • Who is supposed to do what by when is in a group chat, scrolled past by lunch.

So the real problem is not the accounting software. It is that the business has no shared operational record, and the books are the only structured system in the building.

Signs you have outgrown spreadsheets for operations

If three or more of these are true every week, the cost of the current way is probably higher than you think:

  • There are several versions of the same sheet, and nobody is sure which is current.
  • Stock in the file does not match stock in the godown, and a physical count is the only way to know.
  • “Where is my order?” needs a phone call to dispatch before you can answer the customer.
  • Two people quote the same customer two different outstanding amounts.
  • Month-end reconciliation between sales, stock and accounts takes days.
  • You, the owner, are the only person who knows the full picture — and you cannot take a week off.
  • Every new staff member adds confusion instead of capacity.

If none of these sound familiar, you may not need anything new. A tidy shared sheet and a clear process can take a business surprisingly far.

How to move, step by step — whatever you choose

These steps apply whether you end up with BOOM, another tool, or simply a better-structured set of sheets.

  1. Write down what stays in accounting. Books, GST returns, e-invoicing, e-way bills, TDS, statutory reports. Your accountant keeps all of it. Nothing about fixing operations should disturb compliance.
  2. Map the daily operational flows. For order intake, dispatch, stock, purchases, collections and tasks: who does what, in which tool, and where the information goes next. A whiteboard and an hour with your team is enough.
  3. Find the numbers that disagree. Stock counts, customer dues, order status, supplier balances. The places where three sources give three answers are where money is leaking.
  4. Define the handoff to accounts. Decide what the accountant needs from operations — sales, purchases, payments received and made — in what format, how often, and who checks that the two sides agree.
  5. Clean master data. One name and code for every customer, product and supplier, used the same way in operations and in accounts. This is dull work and it matters more than any software choice.
  6. Move in phases. Orders and stock first, because they touch everyone. Then customer and supplier dues. Then portals, tasks and reporting. Switching everything on one Monday is how rollouts fail.
  7. Run in parallel, then retire the sheets. Keep the old sheet going for a short, fixed period, compare numbers, fix gaps, and then stop maintaining it. If both are kept “just in case”, the team will trust neither.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to stop using Tally if I move to BOOM?

No. BOOM is an operations layer for orders, stock, purchases, dues, tasks and profit visibility. Tally or your existing accounting software stays with your accountant for books, GST and statutory reporting.

How do I know my business has outgrown Excel for operations?

Common signs are several versions of the same sheet, stock counts that do not match the godown, order status that needs a phone call to find out, customer dues that different people quote differently, and an owner who is the only person who knows the full picture.

Can BOOM file GST returns or generate e-invoices and e-way bills?

No. BOOM does not do GST return filing, e-invoicing, e-way bills or statutory accounting. Those stay in your accounting software. BOOM handles daily operations and can export data for your accountant.

How does data get from BOOM to my accountant?

BOOM supports data export, so orders, purchases and payments can be shared with your accountant on an agreed schedule. The exact format and handoff are agreed during scoping so they match how your accountant works.
